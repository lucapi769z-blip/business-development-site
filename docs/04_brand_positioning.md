# 1. Purpose of This Document

This document defines how [Brand] should be understood, by whom, and in what voice.

It establishes:

- the brand idea and its role;
- what the brand promises and what it does not;
- why a client would choose it;
- how it speaks to each audience;
- how it differs from adjacent categories;
- the fixed order of its messages;
- its tone and vocabulary in English and Italian;
- the rules governing claims and trust;
- the criteria for a future brand name and positioning line;
- a test every future message must pass.

It governs all future brand communication: website, proposals, email, company profile, institutional correspondence and publishing communications.

# 2. Approved Positioning Model

The positioning uses three territories, each with one distinct role.

| Role | Territory | What it governs | What it must NOT do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brand idea | B — International Opportunity Development | Why the brand exists and what it fundamentally does | Become a public service name, or drift into vague "opportunity" or deal-making language |
| Register | A — Institutional International Business Development | How the brand sounds: restraint, precision, discretion, seriousness | Imply scale: departments, offices, permanent teams, accreditation, public authority or official mandates |
| Operational language | C — Strategic Market & Partnership Development | How services are described: concrete, operational, clear | Become the overall brand idea or reduce the brand to an export consultancy |

## Rules that keep the roles distinct

1. Territory B never sets the tone. Territory A never sets the claims. Territory C never becomes the headline idea.
2. "Opportunity Development" is an internal idea. The public service category remains **Business Development**.
3. In descriptive positioning statements, "opportunity" should normally be qualified or made concrete by the surrounding context: a market-entry opportunity, a selected commercial opportunity, a publishing opportunity. Approved editorial or CTA concepts may use "opportunity" alone where the context makes the meaning clear. The following are explicitly compliant: "Discuss an Opportunity" and "Not every opportunity is the right opportunity."
4. Service descriptions use Territory C vocabulary: market entry, counterparties, distributors, importers, licensees, scope, process.

## Approved strategic positioning formula

Strategic basis, not website copy:

> For international companies approaching Italy, and selected Italian companies, entrepreneurs, publishers, authors and institutional organisations developing relevant commercial, international or publishing initiatives,
>
> [Brand] is an independent, Founder-led international business development brand
>
> that identifies and develops selected opportunities, determines relevant counterparties and works to create the conditions for meaningful business conversations,
>
> with the restraint, precision and discretion expected by established and institutional counterparts,
>
> through a defined scope, a selective approach and the Founder's verified experience across international trade, entrepreneurship, commercial development, digital projects and publishing.

### Reading notes

- The order of audiences in the formula reflects the priority of document 03, section 2: international companies approaching Italy come first; institutional organisations are a secondary audience.
- "Works to create the conditions for" is deliberate. The brand develops conditions and opportunities. It does not guarantee responses, meetings, introductions, partnerships or commercial outcomes.
- "International business development" is used descriptively, in lower case. It is not a separate service (document 02, section 2).
- The Founder's experience sequence is international trade, entrepreneurship, commercial development, digital projects and publishing. Business Development is the present professional evolution built on that experience.

# 3. Brand Essence

## Essence

Internal formulation, not a tagline:

> Selected opportunities, developed with judgement.

## Expanded essence

[Brand] exists to recognise which opportunities are worth developing, to identify who should be part of them, and to do the disciplined work that makes a serious business conversation possible.

Its value is not information alone. Its value is judgement applied to a specific opportunity, combined with the work needed to move it forward.

## Brand attributes

The brand should be perceived as:

- independent;
- international;
- Founder-led;
- selective;
- commercially aware;
- institutionally credible;
- pragmatic;
- discreet;
- precise;
- relationship-oriented;
- entrepreneurial;
- contemporary;
- trustworthy.

The brand must NOT be perceived as:

- a large consultancy;
- a marketing agency;
- a commercial agent;
- a broker;
- a lobbying firm;
- a public affairs firm;
- an investment bank;
- a startup;
- an influencer brand;
- a luxury lifestyle brand;
- an experimental creative studio.

# 4. Brand Role

[Brand] plays the role of a **professional counterpart for business development**:

- for international companies, a professional counterpart on the Italian side;
- for selected Italian companies, a professional counterpart for selected cross-border initiatives;
- for publishers and authors, a selective counterpart for publishing and rights opportunities;
- for institutional organisations, a credible counterpart for initiatives within the existing services.

## What the role is

- evaluating whether an opportunity is worth developing;
- defining the scope of the work;
- identifying relevant counterparties;
- conducting selected outreach where agreed;
- supporting early business conversations;
- following up with discipline.

## What the role is NOT

- a guaranteed route to market;
- the client's legal representative or commercial agent;
- a party with authority to bind the client;
- a provider of legal, tax, financial or regulatory advice;
- a substitute for the client's own decision-making and negotiation.

# 5. Brand Promise

## Principle

The brand does not promise outcomes.

## Internal brand promise

[Brand] promises:

- **careful evaluation** — every opportunity is considered on its merits before any commitment;
- **selective engagement** — work is accepted only where the brand can realistically contribute;
- **clear scope** — what will and will not be done is agreed before substantive work begins;
- **relevant research and identification, where agreed** — focused on the agreed objective, never presented as due diligence;
- **professional outreach, where agreed** — conducted in the brand's own professional capacity, without false identity or implied authority (document 02, section 5);
- **disciplined follow-up** — within the agreed scope;
- **transparency about limits** — what is not guaranteed is stated plainly;
- **personal accountability** — the Founder remains responsible for the overall relationship within the agreed scope.

## Usage

- This promise is internal. It informs copy, proposals and conduct.
- It must not be turned into exaggerated marketing language.
- It may be expressed publicly only in plain, factual terms.

# 6. Value Proposition

## The question

Why would a client choose [Brand]?

## Core reasons

1. **Selective rather than mass-market engagement.** Each opportunity receives real attention because not every opportunity is accepted.
2. **Direct Founder involvement.** The person who evaluates the opportunity is the person accountable for it.
3. **International orientation.** The brand is built around the movement of business between international markets and Italy.
4. **Cross-sector flexibility without pretending universal expertise.** Areas of interest are declared; other opportunities are evaluated case by case.
5. **Business development connected with publishing and rights.** A rare combination grounded in the Founder's verified experience.
6. **Structured scope and process.** One canonical process; substantive work begins only after scope and engagement are agreed (document 03, section 15).
7. **Institutional-level communication.** Proposals, correspondence and the website are written to withstand institutional scrutiny.
8. **Specialists involved case by case.** Independent professionals or sector specialists may be involved where a project requires specific expertise, without implying an established network.
9. **Clear boundaries and no guaranteed-outcome claims.** Clients know what they are commissioning and what they are not.

## Value proposition in one internal sentence

> A selective, Founder-led counterpart that evaluates opportunities honestly, defines the work clearly, and does the disciplined business-development work needed to create the conditions for meaningful business conversations.

# 11. Differentiation

## Principle

This section is internal. It explains what makes [Brand] different. It is NOT a basis for public comparison.

Public copy must never attack, name or disparage other categories or organisations. The difference is shown through what the brand says about itself.

## Key distinction

> [Brand] develops selected opportunities through judgement, scope, relevant counterpart identification and structured business-development work.

It does not merely:

- produce reports;
- sell a fixed catalogue;
- make introductions for a fee;
- provide generic marketing;
- claim universal market expertise.

## Differentiation by category

| Category | What it typically does | How [Brand] differs | Risk of confusion and how to avoid it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Traditional consulting firms | Analysis, recommendations, reports; often scale-based | Analysis serves a specific opportunity and leads to counterpart identification and business conversations; Founder-led, selective | Avoid "advisory", "solutions", "insights" as headline language; describe concrete work |
| 2. Commercial agents | Represent a principal and sell on its behalf, often under a regulated agency relationship | Does not represent the client, does not bind it, does not sell on its behalf | Never use "agent", "representative", "on behalf of" (document 02, section 5) |
| 3. Brokers / finders | Make introductions, typically for a fee per introduction or transaction | Introductions are one possible outcome of scoped work, never the product; no fee-per-introduction positioning | Never use "deal", "broker", "matchmaking", "introductions" as the offer |
| 4. Marketing agencies | Campaigns, communication, brand building, lead generation | Develops commercial relationships, not campaigns or audiences | Avoid "growth", "visibility", "leads", "campaigns" |
| 5. Trade-promotion organisations | Institutional promotion programmes, trade missions, public funding instruments | Independent and private; selective, opportunity-specific work | Never imply institutional status, programmes or public mandate |
| 6. Literary agencies | Represent authors, negotiate publishing contracts | Develops publishing and rights opportunities without a representation role, unless lawfully established | Never use "literary agent", "author representation", "we represent" |
| 7. Generic "internationalisation" consultants | Standardised export or internationalisation packages, often across all sectors and markets | Selective, opportunity-specific, cross-sector without claiming universal expertise; connected with publishing and rights | Avoid "internationalisation" as a headline category, especially in Italian; avoid package language |

# 12. Message Hierarchy

A fixed order that governs all future website copy and, where applicable, proposals and profiles.

## 1. WHAT

Business Development between Italy and international markets.

## 2. HOW

Selective, Founder-led, defined process, relevant counterpart identification, selected outreach where agreed.

## 3. WHY TRUST

Verified experience, clear boundaries, institutional-level communication, direct accountability.

## 4. WHAT WE DO NOT PROMISE

No guaranteed responses, meetings, introductions, partnerships or commercial results.

## Application rules

- Messages are introduced in this order. A later message must not appear before an earlier one has been established.
- Each page should be readable in this order, even if not every level appears on every page.
- Level 4 is stated calmly and proportionately. It belongs to the process, boundaries, qualified-opportunities and post-submission content, not to the Hero. It must never dominate a page or sound defensive.
- The international direction (International → Italy first, Italy → International second) is part of level 1.

# 13. Tone of Voice

## Desired tone

- clear;
- measured;
- authoritative;
- concise;
- professional;
- warm without informality;
- direct without aggressiveness;
- confident without arrogance;
- international;
- document-like when appropriate.

## Avoid

- hype;
- startup language;
- motivational slogans;
- sales pressure;
- exaggerated confidence;
- vague institutional jargon;
- luxury-brand language;
- overly legalistic tone.

## Writing principles

- one idea per sentence where possible;
- concrete nouns and verbs over abstractions;
- state limits plainly, once, without repetition or apology;
- prefer the specific counterparty ("distributors", "publishers") to the generic ("partners") where the context allows;
- no rhetorical questions in sequence, no exclamation marks;
- no superlatives without objective evidence (document 01, section 15).

## Tone by channel

| Channel | Register | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Website | Clear, measured, editorial | Short statements, generous spacing, message hierarchy applies |
| Proposals | Document-like, precise | Scope, assumptions, boundaries and outputs stated explicitly (document 02, section 6) |
| Email | Professional, concise, warm without informality | Clear next step; no pressure; Founder identifiable |
| Company profile | Institutional, factual | Verified facts only; Founder-led nature visible |
| Institutional correspondence | Formal, restrained, exact | Correct titles and forms of address; no implied status or relationship |
| Publishing communications | Literate, respectful, precise about rights | No representation language; courteous declines |

# 16. Trust Principles

1. **Verifiability.** Every factual statement can be verified.
2. **Proportion.** Claims are proportionate to what actually exists.
3. **Visibility of the Founder.** The accountable person is named and identifiable.
4. **Transparency of limits.** What is not done and not guaranteed is stated plainly.
5. **Defined scope.** Work begins only after scope and engagement are agreed.
6. **Professional conduct in outreach.** No false identity, no false authority, no misleading information (document 02, section 5).
7. **Confidentiality.** Enquiries, business information and personal data are handled under an appropriate privacy framework (document 02, section 13).
8. **Consistency.** The same positioning, process and boundaries appear across website, proposals and correspondence.
9. **Courtesy in selection.** Declining an opportunity is communicated respectfully.

# 17. Claims Policy

## Allowed claims

Statements that are true, verifiable and proportionate, for example:

- what the brand does, described in Territory C language;
- how it works, consistent with the canonical process;
- who it serves, consistent with document 02;
- the Founder's professional evolution, in verified terms.

## Conditional claims

Allowed only after verification and approval:

- references to specific past activities, projects or collaborations of the Founder;
- names of organisations, publishers or companies the Founder has worked with;
- any description of past results.

Conditions:

- the fact is accurate and can be evidenced;
- the description is not exaggerated;
- naming a third party is appropriate and, where necessary, permitted;
- the claim is approved by the Founder before publication.

## Prohibited claims

- clients, partners, results, numbers or endorsements that do not exist;
- testimonials not genuinely given and approved;
- success rates or statistics;
- offices, teams, departments or presence abroad that do not exist;
- an established network of partners or specialists;
- accreditation, institutional mandates, official endorsement, public authority or official representation, unless verified and true;
- regulated agency, brokerage or representation roles;
- guaranteed outcomes of any kind;
- universal sector expertise;
- superlatives without objective evidence.

# 19. Brand Name Criteria

The final brand name is NOT chosen in this document. It will be selected after audiences, services and positioning are approved (document 01).

## The name should ideally be

- credible internationally;
- pronounceable in English and Italian;
- free of unintended meanings in English and Italian;
- appropriate for institutional readers;
- broad enough for business and publishing;
- not too sector-specific;
- not too startup-like;
- not too luxurious;
- not too abstract;
- not misleading about company size;
- capable of supporting future growth;
- suitable for a professional domain name;
- available for registration and use, subject to later trademark and domain checks.

## Words and constructions to treat with caution

Some words imply scale, status or regulated roles that do not exist:

- "Group", "Partners", "& Associates" — imply multiple partners or a larger organisation;
- "Agency", "Agenzia" — implies an agency role;
- "Institute", "Istituto", "Camera", "Council", "Board" — imply institutional or official status;
- "International", "Global", "Worldwide" — may imply presence abroad;
- "Italia" / "Italy" in an official-sounding construction — may suggest a public or national body;
- "Capital", "Ventures" — imply investment activity;
- "Rights", "Literary" alone — make the name too publishing-specific.

## Naming territories for later exploration

No names are proposed in this document.

| Territory | Description | Strengths | Risks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founder-based | A name derived from the Founder's name | Maximum accountability and authenticity; consistent with Founder-led model | Limits future growth; may read as a personal CV brand, which document 01 rejects |
| Classical / Latin-rooted | A word with Latin or classical roots meaningful in both languages | Pronounceable internationally; can carry Italian identity with restraint; institutionally credible | Can become generic or pompous; many similar names exist |
| Conceptual (judgement, direction, threshold, passage) | A real word evoking the brand idea | Expresses the brand idea; memorable | Risk of abstraction; clichés around "bridge" and "connect" |
| Descriptive compound | A name combining a distinctive element with a descriptive term | Immediately understandable | Generic; weak trademark; limits future growth |
| Coined word | An invented word | Distinctive; available | Too abstract or startup-like if not handled carefully |

# 21. Institutional Credibility Test

## The test

Any future brand message must pass this question:

> "Could this statement appear in a professional proposal sent to a major company, chamber of commerce, foundation or para-institutional organisation without sounding exaggerated, vague or unserious?"

If not, rewrite it.

## Supporting checks

Before approval, each message should also pass:

1. **Verifiability.** Can every fact in it be evidenced?
2. **Scale.** Does it imply a team, offices, network or reach that does not exist?
3. **Guarantee.** Does it imply a guaranteed response, introduction, partnership or result?
4. **Authority.** Does it imply accreditation, mandate, endorsement or representation?
5. **Clarity.** Would a foreign reader understand it on first reading?
6. **Localisation.** Does it work in both Italian and English without literal translation?
7. **Register.** Is it free of hype, startup language and luxury language?

# 22. Future Scalability

The positioning is designed to remain valid as the brand grows.

## What remains constant

- the brand idea: selected opportunities, developed with judgement;
- the register: restraint, precision, discretion;
- the Founder-led accountability;
- the claims policy and trust principles;
- the message hierarchy.

## What may evolve

- "we" becomes more literal if collaborators join; this must be reflected only when it is true;
- specialists or international collaborators may be presented only once they actually exist (document 01, section 18);
- verified case studies may be added (document 03, section 24);
- individual service pages may be introduced without changing the positioning;
- additional languages may be added through localisation.

## Triggers for revisiting this document

- the brand name is chosen;
- a formal legal entity or regulated role is established (for example a lawful representation role in publishing);
- a permanent team or partner network is formed;
- the primary market direction changes;
- a new service is proposed.
