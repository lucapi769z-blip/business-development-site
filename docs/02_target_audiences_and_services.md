# 1. Governing Principles

The following principles apply to every audience and every service.

- Not every enquiry becomes an engagement.
- Opportunities are selected case by case.
- There is no guarantee of commercial success.
- There is no guarantee that a distributor, importer, publisher, buyer, licensee or partner will accept an opportunity.
- Research and identification activities must NOT be presented as due diligence unless a specific professional due-diligence service is separately established.
- The brand does not provide legal, tax, financial or regulatory advice unless appropriately qualified professionals are specifically involved.
- Independent professionals or sector specialists may be involved case by case when a project requires specific expertise. This must NOT imply that an established network currently exists.
- The brand must NOT imply regulated commercial agency, brokerage or representation roles.
- Compensation models are not finalised in this document.
- Existing customers, partners, success rates, networks or geographic coverage must never be invented.
- The brand is Founder-led by Luca Pistonesi. "We" refers to the independent brand and must never imply employees, offices, a permanent team or resources that do not exist.
- The brand must never imply public-sector accreditation, institutional mandates, official endorsement, official institutional relationships, public authority or formal institutional representation, unless such status or relationship actually exists and can be verified.

# 2. Service Architecture

The initial service architecture consists of six services.

1. Business Development
2. Market Entry
3. Partner Search
4. Strategic Partnerships
5. Publishing & Rights Development
6. Author & Publishing Opportunities

Services 1–4 form the general business area.

Services 5–6 form the Publishing & Rights vertical.

No further services are created at this stage. In particular, "International Business Development" is NOT a separate service: it is Business Development applied to international and cross-border opportunities.

The services are designed to be combined. A single engagement may involve more than one service, but each engagement must have one clearly identified primary service.

---

## 2.1 Business Development

### Purpose

To identify, evaluate and develop selected commercial opportunities, and to help turn an opportunity into a concrete business conversation.

Business Development is the broadest service and the brand's core discipline.

It applies equally to domestic, international and cross-border opportunities.

### Typical client

- entrepreneurs developing a new initiative;
- companies evaluating a new product, service or project;
- manufacturers, product and brand owners seeking new commercial directions;
- Italian companies seeking selected international opportunities.

### Activities

- clarification of the commercial objective;
- evaluation of selected products, services and projects with commercial potential;
- identification of the types of parties needed to develop the opportunity;
- structuring the opportunity so it can be presented to potential counterparties;
- development of new business relationships;
- support in early commercial discussions.

### Possible outputs / deliverables

In addition to the minimum engagement documentation (section 6):

- opportunity assessment;
- target map of counterparty types;
- opportunity presentation material, prepared with the client;
- contact / outreach summary.

### Engagement boundaries

- does not include execution of sales on behalf of the client;
- does not include negotiation or signature of agreements on behalf of the client;
- does not include legal, tax, financial or regulatory advice;
- does not include formal business plans or financial projections unless specifically agreed and appropriately supported.

### Relationship to other services

Business Development is the umbrella discipline.

Market Entry, Partner Search and Strategic Partnerships are more specific applications of it.

When an opportunity is not yet clear enough for a more specific service, it starts as Business Development.

---

## 2.2 Market Entry

### Purpose

To support companies in evaluating and approaching the Italian market.

In selected cases, to support Italian companies in approaching selected international opportunities.

### Typical client

- international companies considering Italy;
- foreign manufacturers, product or brand owners seeking an Italian commercial presence through partners;
- in selected cases, Italian companies evaluating a specific foreign opportunity.

### Activities

- preliminary evaluation of whether the target market may be commercially relevant;
- desk research on the market context relevant to the opportunity;
- identification of possible entry routes, such as distributors, importers, commercial partners or licensees;
- definition of target counterparty profiles;
- connection with Partner Search for identification of specific counterparties;
- support in initiating selected business conversations.

### Possible outputs / deliverables

In addition to the minimum engagement documentation (section 6):

- market-entry memo;
- overview of possible entry routes;
- target map of counterparty profiles;
- selected partner shortlist (where combined with Partner Search);
- contact / outreach summary.

### Engagement boundaries

- research is preliminary and must NOT be presented as a formal market study or due diligence;
- does not include regulatory, customs, certification, tax or legal analysis;
- does not include company incorporation, local registration or establishment of a physical presence;
- does not imply that the brand has offices or permanent representatives outside Italy;
- does not guarantee market acceptance, distribution or sales.

### Relationship to other services

Market Entry frames where and how to approach a market.

Partner Search frequently follows Market Entry to identify specific counterparties.

## 2.3 Partner Search

### Purpose

Partner Search answers primarily:

> "Who could be the right commercial or business counterparty?"

Its purpose is to identify potential counterparties relevant to a defined opportunity and, where agreed, to initiate selected contact.

Typical counterparties include:

- distributors;
- importers;
- publishers;
- licensees;
- selected commercial partners.

### Typical client

- foreign manufacturers, product or brand owners seeking Italian distributors, importers or commercial partners;
- international companies entering Italy;
- Italian companies seeking partners abroad;
- publishers seeking counterparties for rights or licensing.

### Activities

- definition of target partner criteria with the client;
- research and identification of potential counterparties;
- preliminary screening of identified counterparties against the agreed criteria;
- selected outreach, only where agreed with the client (see section 5);
- facilitation of introductions and first business conversations.

### Possible outputs / deliverables

In addition to the minimum engagement documentation (section 6):

- agreed partner criteria;
- target map;
- selected partner shortlist, based on available information;
- contact / outreach summary (where outreach is agreed).

### Engagement boundaries

- a shortlist is NOT a due-diligence report and does not certify the reliability, solvency or legal standing of any counterparty;
- outreach takes place only where agreed and on terms approved by the client;
- does not include negotiating or concluding contracts on behalf of the client;
- does not imply acting as the client's commercial agent, broker or representative;
- does not guarantee that any counterparty will respond, accept or sign.

### Relationship to other services

Partner Search is the most operational of the general services.

It may follow Market Entry or Business Development.

It may lead to a Strategic Partnerships engagement, but the two services remain conceptually distinct (see section 2.4).

---

## 2.4 Strategic Partnerships

### Purpose

Strategic Partnerships answers primarily:

> "What could relevant parties build or develop together?"

Its purpose is to help develop collaborations between companies, professionals and organisations whose activities may create value together.

Typical forms include:

- joint initiatives;
- co-development;
- editorial collaborations;
- commercial collaborations;
- complementary business relationships.

### Typical client

- companies seeking complementary partners for a joint initiative;
- Italian companies developing cross-border relationships;
- entrepreneurs requiring a partner to develop an initiative;
- publishers exploring editorial collaborations.

### Activities

- understanding what each potential party brings and seeks;
- identification of possible collaboration models at a conceptual level;
- identification of potential partners, in combination with Partner Search where needed;
- facilitation of initial discussions between parties;
- support in following up early-stage conversations.

### Possible outputs / deliverables

In addition to the minimum engagement documentation (section 6):

- partnership development note;
- profiles of potential partners;
- contact / outreach summary.

### Engagement boundaries

- does not include drafting or negotiating partnership, joint-venture or licensing agreements;
- does not include valuation, investment or financial structuring;
- collaboration models are discussed conceptually; legal structuring requires qualified professionals.

### Distinction from Partner Search

| | Partner Search | Strategic Partnerships |
|---|---|---|
| Core question | Who could be the right counterparty? | What could the parties build together? |
| Focus | Identifying a counterparty | Developing a collaboration |
| Typical result | Shortlist and introductions | Collaboration concept and early discussions |
| Typical examples | Distributors, importers, publishers, licensees, commercial partners | Joint initiatives, co-development, editorial or commercial collaborations |

Partner Search may lead to a Strategic Partnerships engagement. The two services must remain conceptually distinct.

## 2.5 Publishing & Rights Development

### Purpose

To develop selected opportunities involving publishers, works, catalogues, publishing rights, licensing and editorial partnerships.

This service includes, under the same heading:

- International Publishing Opportunities;
- Editorial Partnerships.

### Typical client

- Italian publishers seeking international partners or opportunities;
- foreign publishers interested in the Italian market;
- owners of books, catalogues or editorial intellectual property seeking new forms of development.

### Activities

- understanding the publisher's catalogue, works or project;
- identification of potentially relevant publishers, editorial partners or licensees;
- preparation of presentation material, with the client;
- selected outreach, only where agreed (see section 5);
- facilitation of initial discussions on rights, licensing or editorial partnerships;
- development of special editorial projects in selected cases.

### Possible outputs / deliverables

In addition to the minimum engagement documentation (section 6):

- opportunity assessment for the work, catalogue or project;
- target map of potentially relevant counterparties;
- presentation material prepared with the client;
- contact / outreach summary.

### Engagement boundaries

- does not imply acting as a literary agent, rights agent or regulated representative unless a specific lawful contractual role has been established;
- does not include negotiating or signing rights or licensing agreements on behalf of the client unless a specific lawful contractual role has been established;
- does not include legal assessment of rights ownership or contract terms;
- does not guarantee publication, acquisition of rights, licensing or placement.

### Relationship to other services

This is the publisher-facing and rights-facing part of the Publishing & Rights vertical.

Author & Publishing Opportunities is the author-facing part.

Editorial collaborations of a broader nature may also involve Strategic Partnerships.

## 2.6 Author & Publishing Opportunities

### Purpose

To evaluate, on a selective basis, publishing opportunities for authors and their works, and to help identify relevant publishers or editorial counterparties.

### Typical client

- selected authors with a completed or well-defined work;
- authors with existing works seeking international or rights-related opportunities.

### Activities

- review of the concise author enquiry (see section 9);
- assessment of whether the brand can realistically contribute value;
- where the opportunity is selected, request and evaluation of further materials;
- identification of potentially relevant publishers or editorial counterparties;
- selected contact with counterparties, only where agreed (see section 5);
- exploration of international or rights-related opportunities in selected cases.

### Possible outputs / deliverables

In addition to the minimum engagement documentation (section 6), where an engagement is agreed:

- opportunity assessment of the work;
- target map of potentially relevant publishers or counterparties;
- contact / outreach summary.

### Engagement boundaries

- this service must NOT be called or presented as "Author Representation" at this stage;
- does not imply acting as a literary agent or regulated representative;
- does not include editing, ghostwriting, translation or self-publishing services;
- does not guarantee review, acceptance, introduction to a publisher, publication, a publishing contract, an advance or any placement.

### Relationship to other services

This is the author-facing part of the Publishing & Rights vertical.

Where a work involves rights already published, licensing or catalogue development, it may move into Publishing & Rights Development.

# 3. Target Audiences

The brand serves six primary audiences.

A. International Companies Entering Italy
B. Manufacturers, Product & Brand Owners
C. Italian Companies Seeking International Opportunities
D. Entrepreneurs and Companies Developing New Initiatives
E. Publishers
F. Authors

A secondary institutional audience is defined at the end of this section (see "Secondary Institutional Audience").

Where an enquirer could fit more than one audience, the audience is chosen by the nature of the request:

- a request centred on a specific product or brand and its channels belongs to B;
- a request centred on a company's entry into Italy, beyond a single product, belongs to A;
- a request centred on a company's broader international opportunity or cross-border relationship belongs to C.

---

## A. International Companies Entering Italy

### Who they are

Companies based outside Italy that are evaluating or planning activity in the Italian market.

### Typical situation

The company has an established offer in its home market and is considering Italy, but has limited local relationships or market knowledge.

### Problem / need

- understand whether Italy may be commercially relevant;
- identify potential partners;
- identify distributors, importers or other relevant counterparties;
- initiate selected business conversations.

### Relevant service

- Market Entry (primary)
- Partner Search
- Business Development
- Strategic Partnerships

### What we may do

- discuss the opportunity and the company's objectives;
- once an engagement is agreed, provide a preliminary orientation on possible entry routes;
- define target counterparty profiles;
- identify potential counterparties;
- where agreed, initiate selected outreach and introductions.

### Possible deliverables

- market-entry memo;
- target map;
- selected partner shortlist;
- contact / outreach summary.

### What we do NOT promise

- market success or sales volumes;
- acceptance by any distributor, importer or partner;
- regulatory, customs, tax or legal clearance;
- a formal market study or due diligence.

### When the opportunity should be declined

- the offer cannot lawfully be sold in Italy, or its status is unclear and the client is unwilling to verify it;
- the company expects guaranteed distribution or sales;
- the request requires regulatory or technical expertise the brand cannot responsibly provide;
- the company cannot provide sufficient information about itself or its offer.

---

## B. Manufacturers, Product & Brand Owners

### Who they are

This audience may include:

- foreign manufacturers seeking opportunities in Italy;
- foreign product owners seeking Italian partners;
- Italian manufacturers exploring international opportunities;
- brand owners seeking new channels or commercial relationships.

### Typical situation

The owner has a defined product, product range or brand and wants to reach new buyers, channels or markets through commercial partners rather than direct presence.

### Problem / need

- identify possible distributors, importers or commercial partners;
- evaluate new commercial channels;
- investigate entry into selected new markets;
- create initial market relationships.

### Relevant service

- Partner Search (primary)
- Market Entry
- Business Development

### What we may do

- understand the product or brand, its positioning, current channels and commercial conditions;
- define the profile of a suitable partner or channel;
- identify potential distributors, importers or commercial partners;
- where agreed, initiate selected outreach and facilitate first conversations.

### Possible deliverables

- agreed partner criteria;
- target map;
- selected partner shortlist;
- contact / outreach summary.

### What we do NOT promise

- that any distributor, importer or partner will accept the product or brand;
- new sales, listings, minimum orders, volumes or revenues;
- verification of the reliability or solvency of counterparties;
- brand valuation, licensing valuation or intellectual-property advice.

### When the opportunity should be declined

- ownership of the product or brand, or authority to distribute it, is unclear;
- the product lacks required certifications or compliance for the target market and the owner is unwilling to address this;
- pricing or conditions are clearly unrealistic for the target channel and the owner is unwilling to discuss them;
- the product or brand raises legal, ethical or reputational concerns;
- the brand cannot realistically contribute value in the product category or target market;
- the owner expects guaranteed commercial results.

### Who they are

Companies based in Italy seeking selected opportunities or partners outside Italy.

### Typical situation

The company has a service, capability or offer with potential outside Italy and wants to explore a specific foreign opportunity or cross-border relationship.

### Problem / need

- identify selected international opportunities;
- find potential partners abroad;
- develop cross-border commercial relationships.

### Relevant service

- Business Development, applied to international and cross-border opportunities (primary)
- Partner Search
- Strategic Partnerships

### What we may do

- clarify which international opportunity is being sought and why;
- once an engagement is agreed, identify potential partners abroad through research;
- where agreed, initiate selected contact;
- support early cross-border conversations.

### Possible deliverables

- opportunity assessment;
- target map;
- selected partner shortlist;
- contact / outreach summary.

### What we do NOT promise

- that the brand has offices, permanent networks or representatives in any foreign country;
- local market expertise in every country;
- success in any foreign market.

### When the opportunity should be declined

- the target country or sector is one where the brand cannot responsibly contribute;
- the request is for generic "international expansion" without a defined opportunity;
- the client expects local physical representation.

# 5. Outreach and Client Authority

When conducting agreed outreach, the brand communicates in its own professional capacity.

It may explain that it is exploring an opportunity in connection with a client engagement.

The brand must never imply that it:

- is the legal representative of the client;
- has authority to bind the client;
- may sign agreements for the client;
- is an authorised commercial agent, unless a specific lawful agreement establishes that role.

Where appropriate and agreed with the client, an initial confidential approach may be made without immediately identifying the client.

However:

- no false identity may be used;
- no false authority may be implied;
- no misleading information may be provided.

Final negotiation, legal commitments and signatures remain with the relevant parties and their professional advisers, unless a separately defined lawful mandate establishes otherwise.

# 6. Minimum Engagement Documentation

Every formal paid engagement should normally have at least the following.

## A. Engagement Brief / Scope

Defining:

- objective;
- agreed activities;
- scope boundaries;
- expected outputs;
- relevant assumptions.

## B. Outcome / Closing Summary

Summarising:

- work performed;
- relevant findings or developments;
- agreed outputs;
- possible next steps.

## Service-specific deliverables

Service-specific deliverables may be added depending on the engagement.

Examples may include:

- target map;
- selected partner shortlist;
- opportunity assessment;
- market-entry memo;
- contact / outreach summary;
- partnership development note.

Not every engagement includes every deliverable. The deliverables of each engagement are those agreed in its Engagement Brief / Scope.

# 7. General Operating Process

The process has two distinct phases.

## Phase 1 — Initial Conversation / Qualification

Initial Conversation
→ Opportunity Qualification

The initial qualification conversation may be offered without a professional fee.

Its only purpose is to:

- understand the opportunity at a high level;
- determine whether the request fits the brand;
- identify the likely service;
- decide whether a formal engagement should be proposed.

It must NOT include substantive unpaid work such as:

- market research;
- partner identification;
- market assessment;
- detailed strategic analysis;
- shortlist creation;
- outreach;
- rights research.

Many enquiries end at this stage.

## Phase 2 — Substantive Engagement

Brief
→ Assessment
→ Target Definition
→ Research / Identification
→ Selected Outreach (where agreed)
→ Introduction / Business Conversation
→ Follow-up / Development
→ Completion, continuation or new mandate

Substantive work begins only after scope and engagement terms have been agreed.

### Brief

The objective, agreed activities, scope boundaries, expected outputs and assumptions are agreed in the Engagement Brief / Scope (section 6).

### Assessment

Evaluation of the opportunity and of the brand's realistic role.

### Target Definition

Definition of the profile of the counterparties to be sought.

### Research / Identification

Identification of potential counterparties. This is NOT due diligence.

### Selected Outreach (where agreed)

Contact with selected counterparties, only where agreed with the client and in accordance with section 5.

### Introduction / Business Conversation

Facilitation of first conversations between the client and interested counterparties.

### Follow-up / Development

Support in following up early conversations, within the agreed scope.

### Completion, continuation or new mandate

The engagement ends with an Outcome / Closing Summary (section 6), continues within its scope, or a new engagement is agreed.

Pricing is not defined or published in this document.

# 10. Qualified Opportunity Criteria

An enquiry may be considered a qualified opportunity when, in preliminary terms:

- the company or person is identifiable;
- the product, service, work or project is understandable;
- the commercial objective is realistic;
- the target market or counterparty is identifiable;
- the enquirer has credible ownership of, or authority to discuss, the opportunity;
- sufficient information is available to evaluate the request;
- the activity is lawful;
- the enquirer has a realistic expectation of the brand's role.

Meeting these criteria does not oblige the brand to accept the opportunity.

---

# 11. Reasons to Decline

An opportunity should be declined, or not progressed, in cases such as:

- unclear ownership;
- unrealistic promises required;
- insufficient information;
- unlawful or ethically unacceptable activity;
- request requiring expertise the brand cannot responsibly provide;
- obvious conflict of interest;
- expectation of guaranteed commercial outcome.

Declining should be communicated courteously and without detailed justification where inappropriate.

---

# 12. What Must Never Be Promised

Across all audiences and services, the brand must never promise:

- commercial success, sales or revenue;
- acceptance by any distributor, importer, publisher, buyer, licensee or partner;
- review of every enquiry;
- publication, rights sales or placement;
- funding or investment;
- due diligence, unless a specific professional service is separately established;
- legal, tax, financial or regulatory advice, unless qualified professionals are specifically involved;
- access to an established network, offices or representatives that do not exist;
- regulated agency, brokerage or representation roles.
